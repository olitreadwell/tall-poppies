# Tall People Meetup Wellington launch brand brief

Design read: a community launch poster for tall locals in Wellington, using pub notice and gig poster language. It should feel like a real event with a real venue, not a startup campaign.

## Event facts

- Group: Tall People Meetup Wellington
- Event: launch night
- Date: Wednesday 25 November 2026
- Time: 5pm to 8pm
- Venue: Rosie's Red-Hot Cantina
- Address: 4 Queens Wharf, Wellington Central, Wellington 6011
- Audience: tall people, their mates, and anyone who wants a low-fuss drink
- Entry: all welcome

Fix the Facebook event end time before publishing this kit. The live event page currently says 5pm to 8pm. Every asset here says 5pm to 8pm.

## Positioning

A monthly social for tall people in Wellington. The point is a normal night out without ducking, craning, or explaining your height to a stranger.

The campaign should feel local, dry, and useful. The event details matter more than the brand attitude.

## Audience

Tall locals in their 20s to 40s. They live in Wellington or nearby. They might come alone, with a partner, or with a mate who is not tall.

Do not write as if every reader is tall. Some are friends, partners, or just curious. All welcome means all welcome.

## Voice

- Short sentences. Specific facts. One dry joke at most.
- Use plain words. No motivational language.
- Say the date, time, venue, and address clearly.
- Avoid "finally", "you deserve", "community-driven", "safe space", and "celebrate".
- Do not ask "Are you tall?" Describe the meetup instead.
- Use contractions in social copy. Keep documents plain and direct.

## Visual idea

The mark is a height rule, like the pencil marks on a doorframe. It runs down the left edge of every asset. It gives the campaign a repeatable shape without giraffes, clip art, mascots, or stock photos.

The layout is flat ink on cream. Hard edges. One accent colour. Big type. Lots of empty space. The date is the hero on launch assets. The venue is the hero on the venue card.

No gradients. No glass. No 3D. No glow. No fake grain. No rounded cards. No decorative icons. If an element does not carry a fact, remove it.

Use amber once per asset as a block or rule. Do not make a rainbow.

## Type

- Display: Arial Narrow Bold, uppercase, tight tracking.
- Metadata: Courier New Bold, uppercase, wide tracking.
- Body: Arial Regular.

These are system fonts, so the SVG files open cleanly on macOS. If Arial Narrow is missing on another machine, use Archivo Narrow. If Courier New is missing, use IBM Plex Mono.

Do not convert text to outlines until the final export. Keep text live so the date, venue, and time stay editable.

## Layout

- 1080 by 1350 for Instagram feed.
- 1080 by 1920 for stories and Reel covers.
- 1920 by 1080 for the Facebook event cover.
- 1640 by 624 for the Facebook page cover.
- 640 by 640 for the profile image.
- 72px margin at 1080 width. 120px margin at 1920 width.
- Left align almost everything. The only centred element is a short label inside a block.
- Keep story text inside the middle 70% of the frame. Leave the top and bottom clear for Instagram controls.
- Keep the Facebook page cover text away from the bottom 80px. The profile image overlaps that area.

## Photography

Use a real photo from the night. Candid, full body, high ceiling, people mid-conversation. A phone photo is better than a polished stock image.

Do not use AI faces. Do not use stock photos of smiling groups. Do not use a photo you do not have permission to publish.

If there is no real photo yet, use the type-only assets until you have one.

## Anti-AI checklist

- No purple or blue gradient.
- No glassmorphism or frosted cards.
- No 3D blobs, chrome, or fake depth.
- No generic line icons.
- No centred hero with three feature cards.
- No stock smiling people.
- No giraffe mascot for this launch.
- No emoji confetti.
- No "elevate", "empower", "unleash", "community-driven", or "safe space".
- No rule-of-three list used as rhythm.
- No em dash.
- No "It is not just X, it is Y".
- No fake paper texture or grain overlay.
- No more than one accent colour per asset.
- Use a real venue name and address, not a generic "central city location".

## Editing files

Open the SVG files in Figma, Illustrator, or Affinity Designer. Text stays editable. Replace the date, time, venue, or address. Export at the listed size.

The PNG folder is for direct upload. The SVG folder is the source.

If you edit the SVGs, keep the left rule, the one accent colour, and the hard edges. Those three choices are what make the kit feel like this campaign instead of a template.
